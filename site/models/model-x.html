Tesla Model X service and repair: your questions answered

Short, sourced answers to what Model X owners in the UK ask most: what servicing it needs, what tends to go wrong, what the warranty covers and where to get it fixed.

Last fact-checked: 9 November 2026 by the XKey Developments team. General guidance — always check your own car's details and current official sources. Repair costs vary by car and garage, so we don't quote prices: ask for a written quote.

Servicing

Tesla Model X servicing

Does a Tesla Model X need an annual service?

No fixed one. Tesla lists individual maintenance items for the Model X, each with its own interval, instead of a yearly service1. On an older or high-mileage Model X, a yearly check of suspension, tyres and brakes is still worthwhile.

What maintenance does a Model X need, and when?

  • Brake fluid: health check every 4 years, replaced if necessary1. Tesla says not to top it up yourself.
  • Cabin air filter: every 3 years, and the HEPA and carbon filters every 3 years1.
  • Tyre rotation: every 6,250 miles (10,000 km), or sooner if the tread depth difference between tyres is 1.5 mm or more1.
  • Brake calipers: clean and lubricate every year or 12,500 miles if you drive on roads that are salted in winter1, which covers most of the UK.
  • Wiper blades: replace every year1.

Brake pads, tyres, the low-voltage battery and other wear parts have no fixed interval: they are replaced when worn. See the full Tesla service schedule.

When does a Model X need an MOT?

Like any car, at 3 years old in England, Scotland and Wales, then every year2; in Northern Ireland the first test is at 4 years. There is no emissions test, but tyres, brakes, suspension, lights and steering are all checked. Check when yours is due and its MOT history on GOV.UK. See our Tesla MOT guide.

Common faults

What goes wrong on a Model X?

What goes wrong on an older Model X?

Worn suspension arms and bushes are a known issue on some older Model S and Model X; the car is heavy and has instant torque, so control arms, ball joints and bushes work hard. A clunk under braking, a pull to one side or uneven tyre wear means it should be checked soon. See suspension noise.

Is the Model X covered by the control arm creak bulletin?

Tesla's bulletin SB-20-31-006 for front upper control arm creaks mainly covers 2018–2020 Model 3, but also includes some Model X5. Ask Tesla in the app whether it applies to your VIN.

Why has my Model X touchscreen gone black?

Model Xs built 2016–2018 used an 8GB memory chip in the media control unit that can wear out, leaving the screen blank even after a restart. Tesla recalled these cars in the UK in February 2021 and replaces the chip with a 64GB unit; cars built from March 2018 already had the larger chip3. See touchscreen black.

Does my Model X have a 12V or a 16V battery?

Model Xs built before the 2021 refresh have a 12V lead-acid battery; the 2021-on Model X has a lithium-ion one (usually about 16V).4 The two types are not interchangeable, so ask the garage to confirm from the VIN before buying one. If the low-voltage battery fails the car may not unlock or drive, even with a full main battery. See our 12V / 16V battery guide.

Does my Model X have air suspension?

Many Model S and Model X do. If yours does, turn on Jack Mode before the car is lifted on a ramp or jack, and tell the tyre or MOT garage. See tyre wear and the MOT guide.

Warranty

Model X warranty

How long is the Model X battery warranty?

8 years or 150,000 miles, whichever comes first, with at least 70% of battery capacity retained6.

What does the basic warranty cover?

4 years or 60,000 miles for cars bought after 1 January 2024; older cars keep the terms in force when they were bought. A used Tesla bought from Tesla gets the rest of the basic warranty and then 1 year or 10,000 miles6. See our Tesla warranty guide.

Can an independent garage service my Model X?

Yes, for non-warranty work. Tesla's UK warranty page says you may have the car serviced at a Tesla Service Centre or a non-Tesla service centre, but warranty repairs must be done by Tesla, and Tesla is not responsible for problems caused by improper third-party repair6. In practice an EV specialist can do tyres, brakes, suspension and filters. Keep every invoice. More in our warranty guide.

Repairs

Getting a Model X repaired

Where can I get an out-of-warranty Model X repaired?

A Tesla Service Centre, or an independent EV specialist for work outside the warranty such as suspension, brakes, tyres and the low-voltage battery. Ask whether the garage has worked on your model before, and get a written quote. Compare HEVRA-accredited specialists and independent garages.

Who repairs Model X accident damage?

Your insurer handles accident claims; Tesla's own body repair centre and Tesla-approved body shops can do the work. See bodywork & accident repair and approved body shops.

How do I check a Model X for recalls?

Enter the registration on GOV.UK's vehicle recall checker7. Recall work and service bulletins are booked with Tesla through the app. If you are buying used, also read our used Tesla checklist.
