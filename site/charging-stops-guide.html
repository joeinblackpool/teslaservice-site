Making the most of a charging stop

A 15-30 minute stop doesn't have to be dead time — practical ways to use it well.

Last fact-checked: 28 September 2026. General guidance — always check your own model's specifics and current official sources before relying on it for a decision.

The reality

How long are you actually waiting?

A typical top-up charge on a road trip is realistically 15-30 minutes depending on how much charge you need and the site's charging speed — not the hours some assume from thinking in petrol-stop terms, but long enough to be worth planning for rather than just standing next to the car.

Make it useful

What to actually do with the time

Most UK Supercharger and rapid-charging sites are increasingly sited near retail parks, motorway services or town centres specifically because operators know this. Check what's within a short walk before you arrive using a regular maps app — a coffee, a proper toilet break, or just stretching your legs and switching drivers all turn a charging stop into a normal part of the journey rather than dead time.

Practical tips

A few practical habits

Small things that help:

  • Start navigation to your next stop before you get out of the car, so the route and charge plan are already set when you're back.
  • If travelling with others, use the stop for a driver swap rather than stopping again later just for that.
  • Check the charging app or car screen for an estimated 'done' time and set a phone timer rather than checking repeatedly.
  • For very young children or pets, a scheduled stop is often easier to plan around than an unplanned one — build it into the itinerary rather than treating it as an interruption.
