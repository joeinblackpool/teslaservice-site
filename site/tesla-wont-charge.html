Tesla won't charge or the cable is stuck: what the lights mean

The light around the charge port tells you most of what is going on. Start there, then work through the fixes below. If the cable is locked in, there is a manual release in the boot.

Last fact-checked: 10 October 2026 by the XKey Developments team. General guidance — always check your own car's details and current official sources. Repair costs vary by car and garage, so we don't quote prices: ask for a written quote.

Charge port light

What the colours mean

  • White: ready to charge. Plug in, or remove the connector1.
  • Blinking blue: the car is talking to the charger. Wait a few seconds1.
  • Solid blue: connected but not charging, usually because scheduled charging is set. Turn it off or tap Start Charging1.
  • Blinking green: charging. Solid green: finished1.
  • Amber: the connector isn't fully in. Realign and push it home, check for anything blocking the port, then try a different cable1. Blinking amber can also mean reduced-current AC charging2.
  • Red: a fault has stopped charging. Read the alert on the touchscreen1.
  • No light: the car is locked and asleep. Unlock it or press the button on the charge handle1.

Won't charge

Things to try first

  1. Read the touchscreen alert.
  2. Check scheduled charging isn't delaying the charge1.
  3. Try a different cable or charger, or reset the charging source1.
  4. If it still fails, power the car off and on from the touchscreen and try again1.
  5. After a power cut, charging normally resumes on its own when the power returns2.

In freezing weather the latch can ice up. Precondition the car from the app, or use the inlet heater (Controls > Service) if your car has it2.

Cable stuck

How to release a stuck charge cable

First try the button on the charge handle, the touchscreen or the app. On a Model S, Tesla also suggests holding the boot button on the key fob for 1–2 seconds4. If none of that works:

  1. Make sure the car is not charging. Stop charging on the touchscreen3.
  2. Open the boot.
  3. Find the release cable or tag. On a 2024+ Model 3 it runs through an opening in the left-hand boot wall3. It may be recessed in the trim2.
  4. Pull it down to unlatch the connector, let go, then pull the charge cable out3.

Safety: pull the release first, then remove the cable, never both at the same time, because of the risk of electric shock. Never do this while the car is charging or if any orange high-voltage cables are exposed. Using the release regularly can damage it, so get the fault fixed42.

Next steps

When it needs a garage

A red light that keeps coming back, a charge port door that won't open or close, or a latch that keeps sticking needs diagnosis. A Tesla Service Centre can do this, and so can many independent EV specialists. For home charger problems, contact the company that installed it. See also our home charging guide.
