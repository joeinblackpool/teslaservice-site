EV charging etiquette

The unwritten rules that make shared public charging work better for everyone.

Last fact-checked: 28 September 2026. General guidance — always check your own model's specifics and current official sources before relying on it for a decision.

Idle fees

Why do idle fees exist, and how do they work?

Most rapid and ultra-rapid networks in the UK charge an idle fee once your car has finished charging but is still occupying the bay, specifically to discourage using a charging space as a parking space. Move your car promptly once charging completes — the app or car will usually notify you — both to avoid the fee and to free the bay for the next person.

Sharing the bays

What counts as 'hogging' a charger?

Charging to 100% at a rapid charger when you only need enough to reach your next stop is generally considered poor etiquette at a busy site, since charging speed slows significantly above roughly 80% on most rapid chargers — meaning you occupy the bay for a disproportionately long time for a small amount of extra range. Charging to a higher percentage makes more sense when it's genuinely your last stop before a longer drive, or at a quiet site with no one waiting.

Practical courtesies

A few other practical habits

Things that make shared charging smoother for everyone:

  • Coil the cable back neatly when you're done, rather than leaving it trailing.
  • If a charger is broken, report it through the network's app — it genuinely helps other drivers and speeds up the fix.
  • Don't unplug someone else's car, even if you think it's finished — check the screen or ask first.
  • At a busy site with people waiting, a quick, friendly heads-up on how much longer you need goes a long way.
