Tesla tyres, brakes & routine maintenance

What genuinely wears differently on an EV, how regenerative braking changes brake maintenance, and what a Tesla actually needs versus a petrol car.

Last fact-checked: 28 September 2026. General principles across Tesla's range — always check your own model's specific maintenance recommendations for exact intervals.

Tyre wear

Why do Tesla tyres wear out faster?

Two compounding factors. Instant torque from an electric motor delivers power far more abruptly than a petrol engine's more gradual build-up, which puts more stress on the contact patch every time you accelerate. On top of that, a Tesla is typically heavier than an equivalent-sized petrol car because of the battery pack, and that extra weight accelerates tread wear on its own. The combination means many owners find they're replacing tyres somewhat more often than they were used to on a previous petrol car, particularly if driving style leans toward brisker acceleration.

Choosing tyres

Do I need special EV tyres?

Not strictly — a Tesla will run on standard tyres of the correct size and rating. But many owners specifically choose EV-designed or reinforced tyres, which typically have a stiffer sidewall built to cope with the extra weight and torque, better rolling resistance for range, and in some cases acoustic foam bonded inside the tyre to cut down road noise — more noticeable in an EV without engine noise to mask it. If you're replacing tyres anyway, it's worth asking whichever garage does the job whether an EV-specific option is available for your size before defaulting to a standard equivalent.

Regenerative braking

Does regen braking mean the brakes last longer?

Generally, yes, and often considerably so. Regenerative braking — using the electric motor to slow the car and recover energy back into the battery — handles most everyday deceleration, so the physical friction brakes do far less work than on a petrol car and typically last a lot longer before needing new pads or discs. The trade-off: brakes that are barely used can be more prone to corrosion or the callipers seizing slightly over time, particularly in wet or salted-road winter conditions, so they still benefit from periodic inspection and servicing even when the pads themselves have plenty of life left.

Brake fluid

Do I still need to change the brake fluid?

Yes — this is one maintenance item that doesn't change with an EV. Brake fluid absorbs moisture from the surrounding air over time regardless of how much the brakes are physically used, which gradually lowers its boiling point and effectiveness in an emergency stop. Tesla's own guidance is to have the brake fluid checked, and replaced if needed, roughly every two years, independent of mileage or how lightly the brakes have been used.

The bigger picture

What routine maintenance does a Tesla actually need?

  • No engine oil, oil filter, spark plugs, timing belt or exhaust system — all eliminated compared with a petrol car.
  • Tyre rotation and wear checks, more frequently than you might be used to (see above).
  • Brake fluid check/replacement roughly every two years.
  • Cabin air filter replacement periodically.
  • Occasional coolant system check for the battery and motor cooling loop.
  • Wiper blades and 12V auxiliary battery health, as with any car.

There's no fixed "service schedule" in the traditional sense, but an annual visual inspection at a competent garage — official or independent — is a sensible habit even without a mandated interval forcing it.
