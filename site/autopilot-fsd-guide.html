Autopilot & FSD in the UK: what's actually switched on

What each package legally means for you as the driver, in plain terms — not a marketing description.

Last fact-checked: 27 September 2026. Automated-driving law is actively changing in the UK under the Automated Vehicles Act 2024 framework. This page explains the general legal principle, not a specific feature's current live status — always check GOV.UK and Tesla's own UK site for what's authorised today.

This page is general information, not legal advice, and it isn't a substitute for reading your own owner's manual and the current terms Tesla attaches to any driver-assistance feature on your specific car.

Standard Autopilot

Is Autopilot legal to use in the UK?

Yes. Standard Autopilot — traffic-aware cruise control and lane-centring — is a driver-assistance feature, the same broad category as adaptive cruise control and lane-keep assist offered by many other manufacturers. You remain the driver in the legal sense at all times: hands on the wheel, attention on the road, and liable for the vehicle's behaviour exactly as you would be without it engaged.

Full Self-Driving (Supervised)

Is FSD approved for hands-off driving in the UK?

No. As of this page's last check, no consumer car sold in the UK — including any Tesla — is authorised for genuinely hands-off, driver-not-responsible automated driving on public roads. Tesla's own naming reflects this: the package is called "Full Self-Driving (Supervised)" specifically because supervision and full legal responsibility stay with the driver, wherever the feature is made available. Don't take the product name as a statement of legal permission — check the current position directly with GOV.UK and Tesla before assuming otherwise.

What's the difference

Autopilot vs Enhanced Autopilot vs FSD (Supervised)

  • Autopilot — traffic-aware cruise control and lane centring.
  • Enhanced Autopilot — adds features like automatic lane changes and self-parking on top of standard Autopilot.
  • FSD (Supervised) — the broadest package, aiming to handle more driving situations including junctions and turns, but "Supervised" is doing the legal work in that name: full attention and readiness to intervene is required at all times, regardless of package.

Liability

Who's liable if something goes wrong while it's engaged?

You are. Current UK law treats the human driver as responsible for the vehicle while using any Tesla driver-assistance feature. These systems assist — they don't transfer legal responsibility. That distinction only applies to a vehicle specifically authorised as self-driving under future regulation, and no Tesla on UK roads currently holds that status.

When features get restricted

Why did my car disable Autopilot or FSD features?

Tesla can restrict driver-assistance features if its cabin camera or steering-wheel torque sensors register repeated inattention, or in response to a safety recall or a regulatory requirement. This is a software-level decision made entirely by Tesla — not something a garage, independent or official, has any ability to override.
