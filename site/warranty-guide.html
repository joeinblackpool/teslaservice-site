Tesla warranty in the UK: what's actually covered

Tesla's current UK warranty terms by model: the basic vehicle warranty, battery and drive unit cover, what is excluded, and whether you can use an independent garage. Taken from Tesla's own UK warranty page and warranty documents.

Last fact-checked: 2 November 2026 against Tesla UK, Vehicle Warranty. Terms depend on when and how the car was bought. The warranty supplied with your own car is the one that applies.

Tesla says the current basic warranty applies to vehicles bought after 1 January 2024. Older cars keep the warranty that was in effect when they were bought. Your Tesla Account or the Tesla app shows which terms apply to your car.

Battery and drive unit

Does the Tesla battery warranty cover degradation?

Yes, within limits. Tesla's UK page states each battery and drive unit term "with minimum 70% retention of battery capacity over the warranty period". The cover is for a battery that falls below that level within the term, as well as for outright failure. Normal, gradual capacity loss above 70% is not a warranty fault. What's excluded

What isn't covered, and what can affect a claim

Tesla's UK Used Vehicle Limited Warranty (effective 8 July 2024) lists exclusions, including:

  • "Accidents, collisions, or objects striking the vehicle". Accident damage is a matter for your insurer (see approved body shops and insurance).
  • "Normal wear or deterioration" and "expendable maintenance items (such as wiper blades/inserts, brake pads/linings, filters, etc.)". Tyres carry their own maker's warranty.
  • "Non-genuine Tesla parts or accessories or their installation", and parts, fluids or accessories fitted by a person or facility "not authorized or certified to do so".
  • Hardware or software modifications, including third-party applications.
  • Failure to take the car to a Tesla Service Centre for repairs or service that Tesla has recommended.
  • Charging equipment not supplied with the car. Tesla's UK page adds that damage caused by third-party adaptors or chargers, including bi-directional adaptors, is not covered.
  • The warranty is void if an insurer declares the car a total loss, or if it is branded salvage, rebuilt or similar.

Read the full list in the warranty document for your car: UK New Vehicle Limited Warranty (PDF) · UK Used Vehicle Limited Warranty (PDF).

Independent garages

Does using an independent garage void the Tesla warranty?

Not by itself. Tesla's UK warranty page says warranty repairs must be done at a Tesla Service Centre. For non-warranty work, "you may choose to have your vehicle serviced at a Tesla Service Centre or a non-Tesla service centre". Tesla adds that it is "not responsible for any issues caused by improper repair or maintenance by a third-party service centre". The UK used-vehicle warranty also says Tesla does not require all service to be done at a Tesla Service Centre. However, improper maintenance or repair by others "may" void the warranty or exclude cover for the damage it causes.

In practice, an independent can do tyres, brakes, suspension, cabin filters and similar work, as long as it is done properly. Keep every invoice. Anything that should be a warranty claim goes to Tesla, booked through the app. The wider UK competition rules for car-maker aftermarket agreements are set by the Motor Vehicle Agreements Block Exemption Order 2023, in force from 1 June 2023 until 31 May 2029. The Motor Ombudsman's warranty FAQ lists ignoring the maker's service schedule and fitting non-genuine or non-approved parts among the things that can affect a claim.

Buying used

Does the Tesla warranty transfer to the next owner?

Tesla's UK used-vehicle warranty is "transferable at no cost to any private retail purchaser" who buys the car from a previous private owner while the warranty is still running. The car must be registered in the new owner's name. Before you buy, check the car's warranty start date and remaining cover with Tesla rather than relying on the seller. Checking your own car

How to check your car's warranty and make a claim

  • See your car's warranty terms and status in your Tesla Account or the Tesla app.
  • To make a claim, book through the Tesla app (Service, then describe the fault). Warranty work is done at a Tesla service centre or by Tesla Mobile Service.
  • Keep every service and repair invoice, whichever garage did the work.
