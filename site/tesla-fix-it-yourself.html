Tesla fix it yourself: what UK owners can safely repair at home

A lot of the faults owners ask about on forums need no garage at all: a reset, a calibration, a filter or a frozen handle. Others look easy online but involve high voltage, brakes or steering and belong with a professional. This page sorts the two, using Tesla's own owner guides wherever they exist.

Last fact-checked: 9 November 2026 by the XKey Developments team. Steps are summarised from Tesla's published do-it-yourself guides and owner's manuals (linked below). Some of those are written for other countries, so always open the guide for your exact model and build date first. We don't quote repair prices: ask a garage for a written quote.

Before you touch anything

  • Never touch, cut or move orange cables. Orange means high voltage (several hundred volts). Tesla's own filter guide warns that damaging them can cause "serious injury or death"1.
  • Unplug the charge cable before working on or under the car, even if it isn't charging11.
  • Brakes, steering, suspension and the high-voltage battery are not DIY jobs. They're safety-critical, are checked at the MOT, and mistakes can kill.
  • Warranty: Tesla's manual says damage caused by maintenance or repairs done by non-Tesla-certified technicians isn't covered11. If your car is still in warranty, check before you start (warranty guide).
  • Work with the car in Park, on level ground, and use proper axle stands (never just a jack) if a wheel comes off.

Musty or "gym bag" smell: replace the cabin filters

Tesla recommends new cabin air filters every 2 years on the Model 3, Model Y and Model S1, 2, 3. On the Model 3 and Model Y there are two filters (upper and lower) behind a cover in the front passenger footwell. The job takes a trim tool and, on most cars, a T20 or 6 mm screwdriver.

  1. Turn the climate off, slide the passenger seat fully back and lift out the floor mat.
  2. Release the footwell cover (clips or magnets) and unplug its two small connectors by the connector, not the wires.
  3. Ease the side panel off the centre console with a trim tool, then remove the filter cover (one screw on older cars; Model Y built from about February 2025 uses squeeze tabs instead)2.
  4. Pull out the upper filter, then the lower one. Fit the new ones lower first, with the arrows pointing to the rear of the car.
  5. Refit everything in reverse, then reset the filter reminder under Controls > Service on the touchscreen.

On the Model 3, orange high-voltage cables are attached near the filter cover. Don't stretch or bend them. If you can't see a filter cover at all, put the trim back and contact Tesla1.

If the smell comes back quickly with new filters, moisture on the A/C evaporator is the usual cause. Owners often use an evaporator foam cleaner here, but that means reaching into the air-con housing near the HV cables, so an air-con specialist is the safer choice. More on intervals and HEPA filters in our cabin filter and A/C smell guide.

Window not sealing, catching the trim or dropping too far: calibrate it

Frameless Tesla windows drop slightly when you open the door and rise into the seal when you shut it. If a window touches the bright trim, won't close properly or drops further than normal, Tesla's fix is a recalibration you can do in a minute4:

  1. Close the door of the affected window.
  2. Sit in the driver's seat and close the driver's door.
  3. Using the driver's door switch, raise that window until it stops, then lower it until it stops, then raise it again until it stops.

Try it a couple of times. If it's still wrong, Tesla's next suggestion is to check the puddle lights, then contact Tesla4. A window that grinds or falls into the door has a failed regulator: that's a door-panel-off job for a garage, as the glass is heavy and frameless glass must be set up correctly to seal.

"Camera blocked or blinded" and Autopilot unavailable

First, clean the cameras: the windscreen camera housing (outside), the side repeaters, the B-pillar cameras and the rear camera. Road salt and condensation are the usual culprits in a UK winter, and the warning often clears once the glass is dry.

If cameras are clean but features stay unavailable, a recalibration can help. Tesla's guide says to go to Controls > Service > Camera Calibration > Clear Calibration, then drive. Calibration normally completes in 20 to 25 miles, best on a multi-lane road with clear lane markings; contact Tesla if it hasn't finished after 100 miles5. Tesla notes that clearing calibration won't fix every camera fault. After a windscreen replacement, see our windscreen and camera calibration guide.

Easy Easy

Frozen screen, Bluetooth dropping out, odd glitches: two resets

1. Restart the touchscreen (Model 3 and Model Y)

Only do this parked, in Park. Hold both steering-wheel scroll buttons until the screen goes black, then wait about 30 seconds. This restarts the screen only, not the rest of the car6.

2. Power the car off

If the screen restart doesn't help, go to Controls > Safety > Power Off and wait at least two minutes without touching anything: no doors, no brake pedal, no screen. Then press the brake or open a door to wake it7. If the screen is completely black and won't respond, see Tesla touchscreen black or frozen, which also covers the Model S and X eMMC recall.

Phone key problems are covered separately in phone key troubleshooting.

12V or 16V low-voltage battery warning

When the car warns the low-voltage battery needs replacing, book it in promptly: if it fails the car may not unlock or drive. What you can do yourself depends on the battery type, which you can check under Controls > Software > Additional Vehicle Information9.

  • Lead-acid 12V (Model 3 and Model Y built in Shanghai before about October 2021 or Fremont before about December 2021): Tesla publishes an owner replacement guide8. It's a 10 mm socket job, but the order of steps matters: power the car off, disconnect the negative clamp, and on Model 3 built after about October 2020 also unplug the "first responder loop" before the positive clamp, then reverse it all. Older Model 3 cars use a different procedure under the rear seat instead. That guide is written for North American cars, so check the procedure matches yours, and fit the same type of battery, as Tesla warns a different type can cause damage not covered by warranty.
  • Lithium-ion low-voltage battery (later Model 3 and Model Y, and most newer cars, including 16V systems): Tesla's owner guides don't include a DIY replacement for these, so have Tesla or an EV specialist do it.

Car completely dead? How to open the frunk

On the Model 3 and Model Y the bonnet release has its own wires behind the front tow-eye cover. Press the top right edge of the cover until it pivots, pull out the red and black wires, and connect a portable jump starter: red (+) first, then black (−). The bonnet releases straight away. Disconnect black first, and don't leave the power connected for more than 30 seconds. These terminals only release the bonnet; they don't charge the battery10. Never use a Tesla to jump start another car9.

Tesla also warns: connect an external low-voltage supply before opening a door on a car with no power, or you risk breaking a frameless window12.

More detail in Tesla 12V or 16V battery warning and 12V battery failure: warning signs.

Model S door handle won't present

The motorised handles on older Model S cars are a well-known weak point. Common causes are a failed microswitch or a broken part in the small gear mechanism inside the handle. UK specialist KMC Electric names the switch contact as the most frequent root cause, and warns that forcing a stuck handle can turn a cheap switch repair into a full handle replacement17.

Owners with trim-removal experience do replace switches or fit aftermarket gear kits themselves, but it means taking the door card off, and a wrongly-seated handle can leave the door unopenable from outside. If you're not confident, a Tesla specialist can usually repair the handle rather than replace the whole assembly. If you're stuck inside or out, see Tesla door won't open: the manual release.

Frozen door handles, charge port and wipers

  • Precondition first. Tesla suggests turning on climate 30 to 45 minutes before you leave, from the app or by scheduling it12.
  • Frozen handle (Model 3 and Model Y): on black handles, press hard on the front part to rock it in, then press the rear part to open. On silver handles, take off rings and bump the rear part with the bottom of your fist in a circular motion. Never hard enough to dent the door, and never use tools12.
  • Prevent it: Tesla suggests a little WD-40 on the door handle pivot pins before the cold sets in12, 16.
  • Frozen charge cable: try Controls > Service > Inlet Heater, or Defrost Car in the app, then wait several minutes before the manual release12.
  • Wipers: if snow or ice is forecast, use Controls > Service > Wiper Service Mode to lift them off the glass, and turn off mirror auto-fold so mirrors don't freeze folded12.

Charging: stuck cable, slow Supercharging, overnight drain

Charge cable stuck in the port

Stop charging first. On the Model 3 there's a release tag inside the left-hand side of the boot: pull it down to release the cable, then remove the cable. Always pull the release first, never both at once14. On the Model S, the release cable is in the boot too; Tesla says to use it only when normal methods fail, and never while charging or if any orange conductor is exposed13. Step-by-step with the light colours in Tesla won't charge or cable stuck.

Supercharging slower than expected

A cold battery charges slowly. Set the Supercharger as your destination in the car's own navigation so it preheats the battery on the way; Tesla suggests doing this at least 30 to 45 minutes before arrival in cold weather12. Plan routes with our road trip charging planner.

Losing range while parked

Sentry Mode and Cabin Overheat Protection both keep the car awake, as do third-party apps that poll the car. Turn Sentry off at home and work and check which apps are connected. See the Sentry Mode guide and range and battery guide.

Battery or charging alerts

Some forums suggest running the high-voltage battery down below 10% and leaving it to "recalibrate" when you see a battery management alert. We don't recommend it: deliberately running the battery very low risks running out of range, and Tesla says low-voltage battery damage caused by running out of range isn't covered by warranty8. Note the alert code, keep charging normally and book a diagnostic if it persists. The same goes for a sudden drop in home charging speed, which can point to a fault in the onboard charger, a high-voltage part.

Tyre rotation and wear

Tesla's schedule is to rotate tyres every 6,250 miles, or sooner if the tread depth differs by 1.5 mm or more between tyres11, 15. Staggered setups (wider rear tyres) can only swap side to side, and directional tyres only front to back15. Teslas are heavy, so you need a jack, the correct lifting pads, axle stands and a torque wrench; most owners leave it to a tyre fitter.

Tesla suggests replacing a tyre at 3 mm of tread15, well before the UK legal minimum of 1.6 mm. Check the inner edges: they can wear through while the outside looks fine. See Tesla tyre wear and tyres and brakes guide.

In areas with salted winter roads Tesla also lists cleaning and lubricating the brake calipers every year or 12,500 miles11. That's a brake job, so use a garage: sticking calipers and rusty discs.

Other small jobs owners can do

  • Wiper blades (Tesla lists them yearly), washer fluid top-up, washer jet cleaning, key fob battery and tyre pressures: all have Tesla owner guides16.
  • Water in the boot or spare wheel well: check the drain channels around the boot lid and rear glass for leaves, and that the seals sit flat. If water keeps coming in, a bodyshop can find the leak (bodywork and accident repair).
  • Misting in a headlight: light misting that clears as the lamp warms up is common. Water pooling inside the lens, or misting that affects the beam, needs a repair, and the lights must work properly for the MOT. Resealing a lamp yourself rarely lasts.
  • Rattles and squeaks from interior trim: felt tape on rubbing clips is a safe fix. A squeak from the steering column or a creak over bumps usually isn't trim; see below.

Leave to a professional Pro

Jobs that look easy online but aren't safe DIY

  • Front suspension creaks and clunks. On the Model 3 and Model Y these usually come from worn control arm bushes or ball joints18. Forum tips about injecting grease through the ball-joint boot don't fix a worn joint and can split the boot. A worn arm should be replaced, and the car aligned. See Tesla suspension creak or knock.
  • Tie rods and steering parts. Safety-critical and an MOT item. Fitting them needs correct torques and a full alignment straight after.
  • Air suspension (Model S and X). A corner that sags overnight usually means a leaking air strut or a tired compressor. The system holds high pressure, so leave it to an EV specialist.
  • No cabin heat. The cabin heater on many Teslas runs on high voltage. Don't remove dashboards or "discharge" anything yourself.
  • Onboard charger (PCS) faults, pyro fuse, contactors, drive units and the main battery. All high voltage. Only trained EV technicians with isolation equipment should work on them.
  • Frunk latch warnings. If the car says the frunk is open while driving, stop safely and check it's shut. The latch is a safety part, so have a garage look rather than adjusting it.
  • Brake fluid. Tesla's manual says not to top it up yourself and to have it checked by Tesla or a professional11.
  • Rear camera that flickers when the boot opens. This can be a damaged wire in the boot hinge loom. Taping over it is a stopgap; an auto-electrician can repair it properly.

Tools

Tesla's own help for DIYers

  • Tesla's do-it-yourself guides: free step-by-step owner guides for each model on Tesla's service website, covering filters, wipers, calibration, jump starting and more16. Pick your model and build date.
  • Maintenance reminders: Controls > Service > Maintenance shows what's due and lets you reset an item once done11. Our Tesla service schedule lists the intervals.
  • Service Mode: Tesla's service manual explains how to open it: Controls > Software, touch and hold the word "MODEL" for 2 seconds, then type service19. It shows alerts and system data, but it changes how the car behaves (Tesla says it limits speed and torque) and some routines are only for use during a specific repair20. Look, don't drive, and exit it from the Service Mode menu when you're done. Deeper diagnostics need Tesla's paid Toolbox subscription.
  • Not sure if it's normal? Try our symptom checker and known issues by model, or ask in ask an owner.
