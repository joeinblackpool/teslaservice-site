Using non-Tesla chargers in the UK

Which networks work, what adapter you need, and what's genuinely different from Supercharging.

Last fact-checked: 28 September 2026. General guidance — always check your own model's specifics and current official sources before relying on it for a decision.

The basics

Can a Tesla actually use non-Tesla chargers?

Yes. UK-market Teslas use the CCS (Combined Charging System) connector as standard, the same connector used by the vast majority of other EVs and public rapid/ultra-rapid chargers in the UK — networks like Ionity, InstaVolt, GRIDSERVE, BP Pulse and Osprey are all directly compatible without an adapter. For slower public and destination chargers using a Type 2 connector, a standard cable (often carried in the car) covers that too.

What's different

What's genuinely different from Supercharging?

The main practical differences are payment (most third-party networks need their own app or a contactless card rather than being built into the Tesla navigation experience the way Supercharging is), and route-planning integration — Tesla's own trip planner prioritises and shows live Supercharger availability directly, while third-party chargers may need a separate app like Zapmap to check status and availability in advance.

Practical tips

Making it work smoothly

A few practical points:

  • Download the apps for major networks (Zapmap is a good aggregator to start with) before you need them, not when you're already low on charge.
  • Contactless payment is now mandatory on new rapid chargers under UK regulations, so many no longer require an app at all for one-off use.
  • Check real-world reliability via Zapmap's live status feed where available — a charger showing as operational in an app isn't always guaranteed working on arrival.
