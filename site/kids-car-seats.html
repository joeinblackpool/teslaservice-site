Kids & car seats

Practical fitting guidance and what to check before buying a specific seat.

Last fact-checked: 28 September 2026. General guidance — always check your own model's specifics and current official sources before relying on it for a decision.

The basics

What car seat fittings does a Tesla have?

UK-spec Teslas are fitted with standard ISOFIX anchor points in the rear outboard seats, and most models also support a top-tether connection, meeting the same UK/EU child seat regulations as any other car sold here. Models with an optional third row have their own specific space and access considerations worth checking against your specific seat's dimensions before buying, since third-row access and space can differ noticeably from the main cabin.

What to check before buying a seat

What to verify before purchasing a specific car seat

Rather than assume any ISOFIX-compatible seat will fit comfortably, check the seat manufacturer's own compatibility list for your specific Tesla model and seating position where available, and — if possible — test fit before buying, particularly for bulkier seats in a smaller vehicle interior or the third row of models that have one.

Practical tips

A few practical tips from common owner experience

Things that help in practice:

  • The frunk (front boot) can be a genuinely useful spot for a folded pushchair on models where it's large enough, worth checking against your specific pushchair's folded dimensions.
  • Sunshades for rear windows are a popular, simple addition given the amount of glass in some models' panoramic roofs.
  • If using a third row, check both legroom for the specific ages involved and how straightforward access in and out actually is in practice, not just on paper.
