Frunk & boot space: what actually fits

Practical, real-world packing guidance rather than just headline litre figures.

Last fact-checked: 28 September 2026. General guidance — always check your own model's specifics and current official sources before relying on it for a decision.

The frunk

What's the frunk actually good for?

The front trunk ('frunk') varies considerably in size across Tesla's range, and its usefulness depends heavily on shape as much as raw volume — a shallower, wider frunk suits flat items like a folded pushchair or a soft bag better than a tall, narrow one. Check your specific model's official frunk dimensions against the folded size of anything specific you're hoping to fit, like a particular pushchair, rather than relying on a general 'it's roomy' impression.

The main boot

What genuinely fits in the main boot

Rear boot space and shape also vary by model, with fold-flat rear seats on most models substantially increasing usable space for larger items when needed. For specific common items like golf clubs, larger suitcases, or bulky pushchairs, checking official published cargo dimensions for your exact model — including with seats up versus folded — is more reliable than a general size comparison to a different car type.

Packing tips

Practical packing tips

A few things that help:

  • Soft bags pack more efficiently into an irregularly shaped frunk than rigid suitcases.
  • Check your specific model's official cargo volume figures (seats up and folded) rather than assuming based on the car's exterior size.
  • For a car full of passengers and luggage together, remember frunk space is genuinely separate additional capacity, not a replacement for boot space.
  • If towing (see our towing guide), remember a tow bar can sometimes slightly affect rear cargo access depending on the specific setup.
