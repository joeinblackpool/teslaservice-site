Buying, selling or upgrading a used Tesla

A plain checklist for the three moments that actually matter: buying used, selling or trading in, and deciding whether an upgrade is worth it. No sales pitch, nothing invented.

Last fact-checked: 27 September 2026. Tesla's own trade-in tool, account-transfer process and finance-check providers can change — always confirm current steps directly with Tesla and DVLA before relying on this page alone.

Buying used

What to check before buying a used Tesla

  • Battery health. After a full charge, compare the car's estimated range on the trip screen against the original EPA/WLTP figure for that model and year. Some falloff over time and mileage is normal; a large unexplained drop is a question to ask, not necessarily a dealbreaker.
  • Outstanding finance and history. Run a standard UK vehicle history check (HPI or equivalent) exactly as you would for any car — Teslas are not exempt from finance fraud or clocking.
  • Open recalls. Enter the VIN into GOV.UK's recall checker before agreeing a price.
  • Panel and paint condition. Uneven panel gaps or paint that doesn't match across adjoining panels can indicate prior accident repair — worth a professional inspection if you're unsure.
  • Account transfer readiness. Confirm the seller is willing and able to release the car from their Tesla account. A car that still shows the previous owner's profile isn't fully yours yet.
  • Remaining warranty. Ask for the VIN and check remaining new-vehicle and battery/drive-unit warranty terms — see the FAQ below.

Warranty

Does the warranty transfer to a new owner?

Yes. Tesla's new vehicle limited warranty and the separate battery and drive unit warranty (which typically runs longer and covers more) both transfer automatically for whatever term remains — you don't need to register anything extra. What you do need is the VIN, so you or the seller can check exact remaining coverage either through Tesla directly or the Tesla app once the car is under your account.

Selling or trading in

How to sell or part-exchange your Tesla

There are three realistic routes, each with a different effort-to-price trade-off:

  • Private sale. Usually the best price, but the most effort — you handle viewings, price negotiation, and paperwork yourself.
  • Trade-in through Tesla. If you're ordering a new Tesla, Tesla's own trade-in valuation tool gives an instant estimate applied directly against your new order — convenient, though rarely the top price on the open market.
  • Sell to a used EV specialist. Faster than private sale, typically a middle-ground price, and the dealer handles the DVLA paperwork.

Whichever route you choose: run a battery health check before listing (buyers will ask), gather every service/repair invoice you have as proof of care, and remove the car from your Tesla account/profile only once the sale is complete and paid.

Upgrade or keep

Is it worth upgrading, or should I keep servicing my current car?

There's no single right answer, but three questions cut through most of the decision:

  • How much remaining battery and drive unit warranty is left? A car still well inside that cover carries much less financial risk than one just out of it.
  • What does a real battery health check actually show, rather than how the car "feels" to drive day-to-day?
  • Does the newer model's hardware or software genuinely change how you use the car, or is it a marginal upgrade you'd get for free eventually anyway via an update?

A healthy, still-under-warranty Tesla is almost always cheaper to keep and maintain than to trade in for a new one that starts depreciating from day one. See our running-cost calculator if you want to model the numbers for your own situation.

Paperwork

What paperwork actually needs to change hands?

Two separate transfers, both required:

  • DVLA V5C / new keeper slip — the standard logbook transfer that applies to any UK car sale.
  • Tesla account/digital ownership transfer — Tesla-specific, done through the seller's account or in-person handover process. Easy to forget, and skipping it can leave the previous owner with lingering app access to the vehicle.
